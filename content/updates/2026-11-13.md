<details><summary>English summary</summary>

Microsoft and NVIDIA published Risk Management for Open-Weight Models in Enterprise, a joint resource that presents a shared risk taxonomy and approach for assessing and managing open-weight model risks. Its first paper focuses primarily on the model layer, assessing risk in context rather than by weight openness alone, and notes that evaluating a model alone does not establish the safety of an agent or application.

**Why it matters:** The resource gives engineering, security, legal, safety, and business teams at enterprises deploying open-weight models a starting point for using shared terminology to connect identified risks with evaluation evidence, mitigations, and accountable owners. Because responsibility for safeguards can shift between a managed service and a self-operated deployment, it emphasizes managing risk in the context of the actual deployment configuration, not just the model’s characteristics.

**Key points**
- The paper introduces a shared taxonomy grounded in frameworks including ISO standards and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework. Its five initial categories are Cybersecurity, Data Protection, Content, Human-AI Interaction, and Bias; the taxonomy is not a policy or deployment checklist and should be adapted to each enterprise.
- Risks depend on the model’s use and deployment context, as well as surrounding controls. While the paper focuses mainly on the model layer, assessing an agent or application also requires evaluating permissions, data access, tools, runtime, and operational controls.
- The paper maps evaluation methods to selected risks across the five categories. Microsoft Foundry offers built-in and custom evaluations, automated red teaming, and content-safety controls; teams should verify availability and preview status for their model and deployment, and combine these capabilities with application-specific testing, monitoring, and human oversight.
- Model access and deployment are separate choices, and responsibility for safeguards depends on the service and configuration. Enterprises running their own deployments may take on controls otherwise provided by a managed service; possible safeguards include model changes, access controls, filtering, monitoring, and human review. Future work is intended to extend the approach to runtime, harness, and application layers.

**重要ポイント**
- G7はMicrosoft 365 G5の生産性・コラボレーション、情報保護、セキュリティ、コンプライアンスを基盤とし、Microsoft Entra Suiteでユーザーとエージェント向けの高度なID、アクセスガバナンス、ネットワークセキュリティ機能を加える。
- 一般提供時には、Copilotを使った調査・分析、組織内の知識の検索・要約、Microsoft 365アプリでの文書作成や共同作業を提供する。GA後数カ月には、最新の商用GPTモデル、Work IQの記憶・パーソナライズ、Word・Excel・PowerPointのCopilot編集機能、Copilot Coworkが順次展開される予定。
- GCCで利用可能なResearcherやAnalystから始められ、Agent BuilderやCopilot Studioでエージェントを作成できる。Copilot Connectorsや管理された接続を通じて、承認済みの機関データや業務アプリケーションを利用できる。
- Agent 365は一般提供時にエージェントの発見・識別・ガバナンスを行う共通基盤と、展開・利用を管理する中核的な制御を提供する。セキュリティ、リスク管理、ライフサイクルガバナンスの機能は今後進化する見込みで、GCCでの機能提供状況はサービス説明に掲載される。

<details><summary>English summary</summary>

Microsoft 365 G7 was announced as a government-focused Frontier Suite for GCC environments, bringing together productivity, AI, agents, security, identity, compliance, and governance. G7 and Agent 365 will be available for GCC customers to purchase beginning October 1, with additional purchasing options starting November 1. Capabilities will expand in phases as required GCC authorizations are completed, and customers can begin with experiences available at launch.

**Why it matters:** Government agencies can use this as a foundation for moving from isolated AI experiments toward governed adoption at organizational scale, combining AI in everyday work, mission-focused agents, and management controls. Capabilities will roll out in phases as GCC authorization requirements are met, so government customers can start with available experiences while additional workloads follow.

**Key points**
- G7 builds on Microsoft 365 G5 productivity and collaboration, information protection, security, and compliance, adding Microsoft Entra Suite capabilities for advanced identity, access governance, and network security for users and agents.
- At general availability, Copilot will support research and analysis, finding and synthesizing organizational knowledge, and creating and collaborating on content in Microsoft 365 apps. In the months after GA, the planned rollout includes the latest commercially available GPT models, Work IQ memory and personalization, Copilot editing in Word, Excel, and PowerPoint, and Copilot Cowork.
- Agencies can start with Researcher and Analyst, available in GCC, and create agents with Agent Builder or Copilot Studio. Copilot Connectors and managed connections provide access to agency knowledge, approved data, and line-of-business applications.
- At general availability, Agent 365 will provide a unified foundation to discover, identify, and govern agents, with core controls for managing deployment and use. Security, risk management, and lifecycle governance capabilities are expected to evolve over time; GCC feature availability is listed in the service description.
