<details><summary>English summary</summary>

For Microsoft Azure availability zone design, the article presents a framework that avoids applying three zones uniformly across an entire workload and instead chooses two zones, three zones, or Azure-managed zone redundancy per component. While many components can meet a single-zone failure objective with two zones, quorum-based systems, leader election, and the highest durability targets may require a third failure domain or three-zone placement. It also says cost optimization should come after requirements are defined and that designs should be validated against the actual SKU, tier, region, and configuration in use.

**Why it matters:** This guidance matters for architects and operations teams designing production workloads on Azure because it gives them a way to choose availability zone patterns based on each component’s failure tolerance and ownership boundaries rather than habit. **Key points**
- Azure distinguishes between zone-redundant resources, where Azure handles replication and failover, and zonal resources, where the customer must design multi-zone deployment, routing, replication, failure detection, and recovery.
- Stateless compute, application, and network components can often use two zones if they still meet the single-zone failure objective, so three zones are not inherently required. The article frames two zones not as a compromise but as a simpler option to deploy, test, and operate.
- Stateful systems that use quorum, consensus, or leader election, as well as data stores targeting the highest durability with three replicas, are more likely to need three zones or a third failure domain. It also warns that having three replicas does not help if they are placed across only two zones.
